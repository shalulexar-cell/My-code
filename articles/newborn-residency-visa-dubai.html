Newborn Baby Residence Visa in Dubai: Step-by-Step (2026)

If your baby was born in Dubai, prepare the birth certificate, passport, Emirates ID application and residence permit within the UAE's newborn-document deadline. Here is the practical order of steps.

Quick answer

For a baby born in the UAE to expatriate residents, register the birth, obtain and attest the birth certificate, apply for the baby's passport, then arrange the Emirates ID and residence permit. The UAE Government says resident parents have 120 days from birth to complete newborn documents, including the visa. Birth registration itself should be started within 30 days. Check the official guidance.

Jump to a step
  1. Register the birth
  2. Get and attest the birth certificate
  3. Apply for the baby's passport
  4. Apply for Emirates ID and residence
  5. Check the documents

Step 1: Register your baby's birth

Start with the hospital's birth notification and discharge summary, then follow the relevant Dubai health authority's birth certificate procedure. Keep both parents' passports and the marriage certificate ready. UAE government guidance says residents have up to 30 days to register a birth; do not wait until the 120-day residence deadline to begin this step.

Step 2: Obtain and attest the birth certificate

Check the baby's name, date of birth and parents' details before proceeding. The UAE Government says expatriate residents' birth certificates need the applicable health authority and UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs attestation. Dubai Health Authority's stamp is treated as equivalent to the federal health ministry's seal for this purpose. Ask us to confirm any translation requirements for your case.

Step 3: Apply for the baby's passport

Use the requirements of the embassy or consulate for your baby's nationality. The passport is necessary for the residence application, so check embassy appointment and processing times early. A baby born in Dubai does not automatically receive a UAE passport.

Step 4: Arrange Emirates ID and residence documents

Prepare the baby's passport, photo and birth certificate together with the resident sponsor's passport, UAE residence permit and Emirates ID. The exact supporting documents and sequence depend on the sponsor's status and the Dubai authority handling the application. Children under 18 do not undergo the adult residence medical fitness screening. The Emirates ID application is part of the newborn residence process.

Newborn residence visa document checklist

  • Hospital birth notification and discharge records for the birth certificate stage
  • Baby's UAE birth certificate with required attestations
  • Baby's passport and suitable passport photo
  • Sponsor's passport, valid UAE residence and Emirates ID
  • Mother's identification and residence details, as applicable
  • Any supporting insurance, accommodation or family documents requested for the case
Do not miss the 120-day deadline

The UAE Government currently states that delaying a newborn residence visa beyond 120 days can result in a AED 100-per-day fine and travel restrictions until the situation is regularised. Confirm current fees and your baby's deadline with the relevant authority. Official newborn guidance.

Start with the baby's core documents

Make sure the baby's name and personal details are consistent across the birth certificate, passport and any other supporting records. Differences in spelling can create avoidable delays.

Sponsor documents matter too

The application is tied to the sponsor's status, so current passport, residency, Emirates ID and other sponsor-related documents may be relevant. Requirements vary by case and authority.

Birth certificate preparation

Depending on where the birth certificate was issued and how it will be used, attestation or translation requirements may apply. Confirm the route before paying for extra processing.

Do not rely on an old checklist

Residency procedures can change. A checklist from a friend or an old social-media post may not match your baby's case. Verify the current requirements before submission.

Yes. Sponsor status, document origin and the baby's circumstances can change the required route.
