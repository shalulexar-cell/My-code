Family Visa Requirements in Dubai

Getting your documents right the first time saves time. This checklist covers what is commonly requested, though the authorities decide the final list for your case.

Ask QuickDocs on WhatsApp
Quick answer

There is no single document checklist that fits every Dubai family residence case. The sponsor's status, relationship to the dependent, where documents were issued and whether the applicant is inside or outside the UAE can affect the required documents.

Before collecting documents

Confirm the exact application route first. This prevents unnecessary attestation, translation or repeat document work. For certificates issued abroad, see our UAE attestation guide.

Sponsor documents

  • ✓Passport, residence visa and Emirates ID
  • ✓Tenancy contract or proof of accommodation
  • ✓Salary certificate or employment contract, as applicable
  • ✓Any additional proof requested for your visa category

Spouse documents

  • ✓Passport with enough validity
  • ✓Passport-size photo
  • ✓Attested marriage certificate, with translation if not in Arabic or English

Children's documents

  • ✓Passport and photo for each child
  • ✓Birth certificate, attested and translated where needed

After approval

Depending on the case, the process can include a medical fitness test and an Emirates ID application. Our Emirates ID service helps with the latter.

Check your case

Requirements change and vary by sponsor category. Send us your details and our family visa team will confirm the current checklist.

Frequently asked questions

Do certificates from my home country need attestation?

Usually, documents issued outside the UAE such as marriage and birth certificates are attested before use. We explain the route for your country.

How valid should passports be?

Passports should have sufficient remaining validity. Check before you apply, or ask us to check for you.

Is the checklist the same for everyone?

No. It depends on the sponsor's category and the relationship of the family member.

This page is general information, not legal advice. Requirements, fees and processing times are set by the relevant UAE authorities, differ by case and can change, so we confirm the current position with you before you proceed.
